# COMP3320 – Cyber Security Management in Practice

## AI Use for Data Breach Analysis

This repository contains my research project for **COMP3320 – Cyber Security Management in Practice** at Macquarie University.

The project evaluates the **value, consistency, and accuracy of generative AI when analysing Australian cyber security incidents**. It compares responses from two AI systems across five Australian organisations that experienced data breaches during the first half of 2026.

## Project Objectives

The main objectives of this project were to:

- assess the accuracy of AI-generated cyber security information
- verify references and claims produced by AI systems
- compare repeated responses from the same AI model
- compare responses across different AI models
- analyse reported breach costs and regulatory fines
- investigate ISO/IEC 27001 and other cyber security frameworks
- identify hallucinations, unsupported assumptions, and unreliable references
- evaluate the strengths and limitations of generative AI for cyber security research

## Organisations Analysed

The project examined the following Australian organisations:

1. Victorian Department of Education
2. youX
3. Hazeldenes
4. Champion Homes
5. Scope Systems

Each organisation experienced an initial cyber security incident in Australia between **1 January 2026 and 30 June 2026**.

## AI Systems Compared

Two generative AI systems were used:

- **OpenAI ChatGPT**
- **Google Gemini**

Each AI system was prompted twice for every organisation, producing a total of **20 AI-generated responses** for comparison.

## Analysis Approach

For each organisation, the project examined:

### Reference Accuracy
Every AI-generated reference was reviewed to determine whether it was relevant, accurate, traceable, and capable of supporting the associated claim.

### Response Consistency
Repeated responses from the same AI system were compared to identify differences in:

- breach details
- affected data
- financial estimates
- regulatory fines
- cyber security certifications
- explanations of the attack

### Cross-Model Comparison
ChatGPT and Gemini responses were compared to determine whether the systems produced the same facts, numerical values, references, and conclusions.

### Hallucination and Uncertainty
Special attention was given to cases where AI systems presented:

- estimated financial losses as factual costs
- vague or unverifiable references
- unsupported technical explanations
- assumptions about attack vectors
- incorrect or overstated certification claims

## Key Findings

The project found that generative AI can be useful for quickly gathering background information and identifying areas for further investigation, but its outputs should not be accepted without verification.

Some of the most important findings were:

- AI systems can produce confident claims that are not supported by reliable evidence.
- Repeated prompts can produce different numerical estimates for the same incident.
- AI may provide references that are vague, incorrectly named, difficult to verify, or unrelated to the exact claim.
- Estimated breach costs can be presented in a way that appears similar to confirmed financial losses.
- The absence of a publicly reported fine does not mean that an incident had no financial impact.
- ISO/IEC 27001 certification does not guarantee that an organisation cannot experience a cyber attack.
- Cyber security frameworks improve governance and resilience but still depend on effective implementation, monitoring, and technical controls.
